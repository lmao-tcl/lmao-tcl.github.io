Overview#

lmao.tcl gives your eggdrop the public commands people actually expect in a channel — ops, voices, bans, topic and user management — plus an auto-voice system for people who talk and idle cleanup for ops and voices who don't. Everything is per channel and every command reply is a notice, so the bot never floods the channel it's supposed to be protecting.

Installation#

Drop the script in, source it, rehash. No data files, no dependencies.

shell
# 1. put the script in your scripts directory
cd ~/eggdrop/scripts
wget https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lmao-tcl/lmao/master/lmao.tcl
eggdrop.conf
# 2. source it from eggdrop.conf
source scripts/lmao.tcl
partyline
# 3. reload
.rehash

You should see this in the partyline and the log:

output
[lmao.tcl 6.5.2] - Complete production ready version
Loaded successfully - ready to serve!
Edit the config first. cc(mainchan) and cc(backchan) ship as #mainchan and #secretchan placeholders — set them to your real channels before you rely on !ops.

Access levels#

Access is a position, not a pile of flags: every user sits on exactly one rung. The !add… and !del… commands in access & users move people between rungs and clear whatever they held before. Underneath it is still plain eggdrop flags, global (everywhere) or set on a single channel. Bind masks read global|channel — n|o means "global owner or channel op".

FlagLevelCan do
nOwnerEverything, including bot control (!restart, !addchan, !botnick). Granted on DCC with .chattr, never by command
mMasterModes, blacklist, flags, users, modules, chanlog, !say / !act, !addop
oOpChannel +o, topic, !addmod — everything a Mod can do, plus op and deop
MModKick, ban, unban, bans, invite, voice, devoice — no channel +o. A custom flag, so it never collides with eggdrop's own m
vVoice!voice / !devoice, autovoice, and exempt from ActiveVoice
-RegisteredAny user in the bot's userfile: !info, !whois, !ops, !bot
noneAnyone!help, !showcommands, !verify, !version
Check your own level: !verify shows your handle, level, flags and registered hostmasks. !verify nickname does the same for someone else. !access lists everyone who has a level on the channel.

Access & users#

One command per rung. Granting a level replaces whatever the user had, globally and on the channel, so nothing is ever left behind: !addop on someone who was only voiced moves them up, and !addvoice on an op moves them back down. The bot always says which way it went, and sets the matching mode in the channel.

Only below yourselfYou can never grant a level at or above your own, never change somebody standing level with you or above you, and never change your own access.
Exact level to remove!delop only removes an Op. Pointed at a master it tells you so instead of quietly knocking them down — use !delaccess when you do not care which level it is.
Modes follow access!addop and !addmaster op the user, !addvoice and !addmod voice them, and the !del… commands take those modes back. A demotion from Op to Mod goes out as one -o+v.
A welcome noticeThe person who got access is told by notice who gave it to them. Welcomes are queued and sent one line every cc(access_welcome_delay) seconds.
Unknown nicksA nick with no user record gets one made on the spot from the host the bot can see, and the bot warns you if it is an ISP host instead of a hidden *.users.undernet.org one.
LoggedEvery grant, removal and refused attempt goes to chanlog and the bot's log.

!addvoice

Mod+

Puts someone on the Voice rung (+v) and voices them if they are here: autovoice, and exempt from ActiveVoice's idle devoicer. Replaces any level they already had.

syntax
!addvoice <nick|handle>

!addmod

Op+

Puts someone on the Mod rung (+Mv): kick, ban, unban, bans, invite, voice and devoice, but no channel +o. Voices them, and takes +o off if they had it.

syntax
!addmod <nick|handle>

!addop

Master+

Puts someone on the Op rung (+ov) and ops them if they are here. A Voice or a Mod is moved up; the old level comes off in the same step.

syntax
!addop <nick|handle>
examples
!addvoice dave
[OK] dave is now Voice on #mainchan (flags: -|v)
!addmod dave
[OK] dave moves up from Voice to Mod on #mainchan (flags: -|Mv)
!addop dave
[OK] dave moves up from Mod to Op on #mainchan (flags: -|ov)

!addmaster

Owner

Puts someone on the Master rung (+mov) and ops them. Owner is deliberately not grantable by command — that stays a .chattr job on the party line.

syntax
!addmaster <nick|handle>

!delvoice !delmod !delop !delmaster

Mod+

Takes that exact level away, leaves the user with no access, and removes their +o / +v. The user record itself stays — use !deluser to remove that. Each one needs the same level as the matching !add: Mod+ for !delvoice, Op+ for !delmod, Master+ for !delop, Owner for !delmaster.

syntax
!delmod <nick|handle>

!delaccess

Op+

Removes whichever level somebody holds, without having to know which one it is, and takes +o / +v off them. Still bound by the same rule: you cannot touch anyone at or above your own level.

syntax
!delaccess <nick|handle>

Self-registration#

People can add themselves to the userfile without an op doing it by hand. It takes two steps on purpose, and the bot is pointed at the one thing that actually decides whether host-based identification is safe: which host gets stored.

Step one

syntax
/msg BotNick register [handle]   # handle defaults to your nick

The bot answers with the safety advice, what it would store, and a one-time code:

notice
Read this first: your host is not hidden. It is safer to auth with X and hide it before registering:
  1. /msg x@channels.undernet.org login <account> <password>   (only ever send that to x@channels.undernet.org)
  2. /mode yournick +x     - your host becomes <account>.users.undernet.org
  3. come back and register again
Why: an ISP host like dsl-142-99-1-2.videotron.ca gets handed to someone else when your IP changes, and they would inherit your access here.
---
I would register newguy with the hostmask *!*@dsl-142-99-1-2.videotron.ca and no access flags.
To register please type: /msg BotNick verify pcf957lcnlbl
That code is good for 5 minutes. If you did not ask for this, ignore it - nothing has been saved.

If the person is already +x, that warning is replaced by confirmation that their host is hidden and belongs to their X account.

Step two

syntax
/msg BotNick verify pcf957lcnlbl

The code has to be read out of a notice and typed back, which is what stops a script from registering nicks in bulk. Nothing is written until it comes back.

What the bot checks — twice

Every check from step one runs again at step two, because nick, host and userfile can all change in between.

CheckWhy
Shares a channel with the botOnly people it can actually see. The host comes from getchanhost, never from anything the user sends
Host does not already match a recordfinduser on the real nick!ident@host
Host unchanged since the requestBlocks asking on one IP and confirming on another
Handle free, valid, one word2–register_handle_max characters, no spaces or punctuation
Rate limit per hostregister_cooldown, charged only when a code is really issued
Code matches and has not expiredregister_token_life
No access flags grantedRegistration gets you a record, nothing else — ops grant levels with !addvoice / !addmod / !addop
Why the X advice is not decoration. Host-based identification cuts both ways: anyone sharing a registered host is that user as far as the bot is concerned. A *.users.undernet.org host can only be worn by someone authed to that X account, which is why the bot pushes for it. When a second person on a shared host asks to register, the bot does not guess — it says the host already matches a record and sends them to an op.
Every request, refusal and success is logged and announced to the ops channel through chanlog. Registrations are never silent.

Turning it off

config
set cc(register_enabled) 0

Channel logging#

One audit trail for the channel staff: who got access, who got sanctioned, who registered, who was refused. It goes to a channel of your choosing, per source channel, and it is a module like any other.

It defaults to ON, pointed at cc(backchan), so it works as soon as the script loads. Each channel keeps its own destination — see !chanlog to change it.

The log is an ordinary channel message, not a notice, so it lands in the ops window like anything else, scrolls back normally and survives clients that hide notices. No colour codes anywhere — the [CATEGORY] tag carries the meaning on its own.

What a log looks like

ops channel
[ACCESS]   #canada boss opped newguy
[ACCESS]   #canada boss set the topic: Bienvenue au #Canada
[SANCTION] #canada boss kicked troll - flooding
[SANCTION] #canada boss banned troll (*!*@troll.host) - spam
[SANCTION] #canada auto: deopped someop after 187 minutes idle
[REGISTER] newguy registered as newguy (*!*@dsl-1-2-3.videotron.ca)
[MODULE]   #canada boss disabled module idledevoice
[BOT]      boss reloaded the scripts (rehash)
[DENIED]   troll (*) tried restart by /msg

What gets logged

CategoryEvents
ACCESSop, voice, mode, chattr, adduser, deluser, addvoice/addmod/addop/addmaster and their removals, topic, topicsync, say, act
SANCTIONkick, ban, unban, blacklist, whitelist, deop, devoice, and automatic idle-deops
REGISTERevery registration request, refusal and success
MODULEmodule toggles, idle-deop settings, logging changes
BOTrehash, restart, jump, save, join, part, botnick, global
DENIEDrefused attempts at owner commands, module changes and op by /msg
Deliberately not logged: automatic voice and devoice from ActiveVoice. Those fire on every talker and would bury the events worth reading. Automatic idle-deop is logged, because it is rare and consequential.

What it refuses to do

Logging a channel into itselfRefused — that is a feedback loop
Pointing at a channel the bot is not onRefused, with a nudge to !join it first
Shouting at an empty destinationThe line is dropped rather than bounced by the server
Logging a failureLogging always happens after the action succeeded, so a failed !deop logs nothing

Modules#

Five features can be switched on and off per channel, live, without editing the config. Four start ON; idledeop starts OFF.

ModuleDefaultWhat it does
topicONEnables !topic and !topicsync — storing and re-applying the channel topic
activevoiceONAuto-voices non-registered users when they talk, and tracks their activity
idledevoiceONRemoves voice from non-registered users who have gone idle
idledeopOFFDeops ops idle past the channel's limit
chanlogONSends this channel's audit trail to the ops channel — see channel logging
examples
!module list                          # state of every module here
!disable idledevoice                  # stop devoicing idlers on this channel
!enable idledeop                      # start deopping idle ops here
/msg BotNick disable #chan topic     # privately, for another channel
Not persistent. Module states live in memory. A .rehash or .restart puts every module back to its default. Re-apply your changes after a restart.

ActiveVoice#

ActiveVoice rewards people who actually talk. It exists for non-regulars — anyone who isn't a registered user with voice-worthy flags.

Someone who is not registered with +n, +m, +M or +v talks in the channel.
The bot voices them (+v) and starts their activity clock.
Every activevoice_check_interval seconds (default 300) it sweeps for idlers.
Anyone silent longer than activevoice_idle_minutes (default 180) loses the voice — if the idledevoice module is on.

Who is never touched

+n owners +m masters +M mods +v voiced users channel ops X / W services

The exempt flag list is cc(activevoice_exempt_flags) — global or channel flags both count. Registered regulars keep whatever voice they have; the bot will neither give it nor take it.

Already voiced when the script loads? People who were voiced before the bot started tracking (or who were voiced by X) have no activity record. Rather than devoicing them, the first sweep starts their clock — so they get a full idle period like everyone else.
Want the voicing but not the devoicing? !disable idledevoice leaves auto-voice running and stops the sweep. Turning off activevoice stops both, because the devoicer depends on the tracking data.

Idle deop#

Ops who sit idle for too long lose +o until they come back and ask for it again. It ships OFF — switch it on where you want it.

Turn it on here!enable idledeop
Set the limit!idledeop #channel 120 — minutes, per channel
Default limitcc(idledeop_default_minutes) (180 minutes)
Sweep intervalcc(idledeop_check_interval) (300 seconds)
Master switchcc(idledeop_enabled) in the config — 0 out of the box
Never touch thesecc(deop_exempt) — a list of nicks or handles

Who is never deopped

Service botsEverything in cc(protected_bots) — X and W
The bot itselfObviously
Protected flagsAnyone carrying a flag from cc(protected_flags) — +n and +m
The exempt listAnyone named in cc(deop_exempt), by nick or handle, case-insensitively

The exempt list is checked first, before flags and before the idle clock. It is for the people who actually run the channel: they op themselves on purpose and are meant to keep it, and it works whether or not the bot has a userfile record for them.

Topic system#

The bot remembers the topic you set through it, per channel, and can put it back when someone mangles it.

example
!topic Welcome to #channel - read the rules!   # set + store
# ...someone changes the topic...
!topicsync                                    # put it back
The stored topic lives in memory and is lost on restart, and the topic module must be enabled on the channel.

Protections#

The bot refuses to be turned against the people running it.

Protected flagsUsers with +n or +m can't be deopped, devoiced, kicked or banned through the bot (cc(protected_flags))
Protected botsX and W are never deopped or acted on (cc(protected_bots))
ActiveVoice exemption+n, +m, +M, +v users are invisible to auto-voice and idle-devoice
Hop on deophopondeop / kickondeop react when the bot itself is deopped
Owner re-check on /msgOwner commands sent by /msg re-verify the n flag inside the handler and log refused attempts
Strangers stay quietSomeone the bot has no record for gets refusals by notice, so a stranger can't make the bot talk to the channel

Known limits#

Worth knowing before you go looking for a bug.

Module states reset on reload. !enable and !disable are remembered in memory only — a .rehash or .restart returns every module to its default.
Stored topics reset on reload for the same reason, and so do chanlog destinations — a channel pointed at #ops falls back to cc(backchan) after a restart.
!chanset and !uptime are open to everyone — both are bound n|-, which requires no channel flags. Harmless for !uptime; change the bind to n|m if !chanset should be restricted.
The bot must be opped for op, voice, kick, ban, topic and every sweep. It tells you when it isn't.

Errors are said out loudA registered user who is not high enough is told in the channel which level they are and which one they need. Somebody the bot has no record for gets a notice instead. Every refusal is logged through chanlog.

Self-deop!deop and !devoice on your own nick are handled before the flag protections.
Tighter targetsMode commands check the target is actually on the channel and take only the first word.
